My journey

The path that led me here

Where I come from

Many cultures, one story

My life has been shaped by more than one culture. I grew up in the Middle East, speaking Arabic and Armenian at home. I moved to New Zealand in the early 2000s with my family. Like many migrants, I knew the hope of a fresh start alongside the grief of leaving people and places behind. Those experiences help me understand clients who are finding their way between cultures, or rebuilding their lives somewhere new.

Architecture & urban design

Designing spaces for people

Before becoming a counsellor, I worked in architectural engineering and urban design. That work taught me to listen deeply, and showed me how much our environments and relationships shape our wellbeing. My creative background still informs the way I work with people today.

Becoming a counsellor

Walking alongside people

I completed my Bachelor of Counselling degree at Laidlaw College, and I am a provisional member of the New Zealand Association of Counsellors (NZAC). I believe everyone has a unique story, strengths and capacity for growth, and I value walking alongside people as they navigate life's challenges and explore positive change.

Today

New Dawn Counselling

I work with children, young people and adults, drawing primarily on Person-Centred, Narrative and Strengths-Based approaches. With children, I use developmentally appropriate, creative and play-based methods to support emotional expression and exploration of their experiences.

I work at each person's pace, prioritising trust, emotional safety, and a therapeutic relationship where they feel heard, respected, and accepted. I support people navigating anxiety, grief and loss, family and relationship difficulties, life transitions, questions of identity, and challenging or traumatic experiences.

My practice is grounded in empathy, respect, authenticity and genuine care, recognising each person's unique story, strengths and capacity for growth.

From architecture to counselling

Building spaces where people feel they belong

My background in architecture taught me that space is fundamental to how people experience the world.

In counselling, space is just as important, not built with walls and windows, but through trust, empathy and understanding, allowing you to be yourself, explore your story and grow at your own pace.

My approach

Building on what is already strong in you

At the heart of my work

Strengths-based

Rather than focusing only on what's happened, we notice what's working: the skills, values, relationships and hopes that have carried you this far. Together we build on those, so you leave counselling more aware of your own resilience.

Person-centred

You lead. I offer acceptance, empathy and honesty, trusting that with support you can find your own way forward.

Narrative therapy

We explore the stories you tell about your life, separate you from the problem, and find the stories of strength that get overlooked.

Child-centred play therapy

Play is a child's natural language. In a safe playroom, children work through feelings at their own pace.

What happens in counselling

What to expect

  1. Get in touch

    Call, email or send a message. Share as much or as little as you like.

    We'll talk briefly by phone to see if we're a good fit and find a time that suits.

  3. First session

  4. Moving forward

    We meet regularly at a pace that suits you, and check in along the way.

Privacy & confidentiality

What you share stays with us

Everything you share in counselling is treated as confidential, in line with the New Zealand Association of Counsellors (NZAC) Code of Ethics and the Health Information Privacy Code 2020. Your information is only shared with your consent, except in the rare situations explained here.

Your consent

At our first session I'll explain, in clear and simple language, how counselling works, what it costs, and the extent and limits of confidentiality. You're welcome to ask questions, and you can change your mind or stop counselling at any time.

When information may need to be shared

Confidentiality has a few limits: if there is serious danger to you or someone else, if you're not able to make safe decisions for yourself, or if the law requires it. Wherever it's safe to do so, I'll talk with you first, and only the minimum information needed will be shared.

Your records

Notes are stored securely and kept private. You have the right to see your records and to know how they're kept and who can access them.

Children and young people

Children receive information in a way that suits their age, and their views and consent matter too. I work in partnership with parents and caregivers and share general progress with them, while keeping the details of what a child says private so they feel safe to open up. If a child's safety is at risk, I will always act to protect them.

Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need a referral?

No. You can contact me directly. Referrals from GPs, schools and other professionals are also welcome.

How many sessions will I need?

It's different for everyone. Some people come for a few sessions, others for longer. We'll keep checking in about what's helpful, and you can stop at any time.

Does online counselling work?

Yes. Many people find it just as helpful as meeting in person, and it can be easier to fit around work and family. You'll need a private space and a reliable internet connection.

How do sessions with children work?

I usually meet parents or caregivers first to understand what's happening, then see your child for play-based sessions and keep you updated along the way.

Can we talk in Arabic or Armenian?

Yes. Sessions are available in English, Arabic and Armenian, whichever you're most comfortable in.
